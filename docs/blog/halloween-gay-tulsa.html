Halloween in Gay Tulsa: Where to Go, What to Wear, Who's Hosting

A season this city takes more seriously than you'd guess, and dresses better for than you will.

🕒 7 min read · Published September 23, 2026

October in Tulsa arrives with a specific kind of relief. The heat finally breaks, the patios fill back up, and every gay bar in this city quietly starts treating Halloween like a second Pride. Not the muted, one-pumpkin-on-the-bar kind of Halloween. The kind where the drag queens have been workshopping a horror-movie lip sync since August and will not be talked out of it.

If your only reference point for Halloween in Oklahoma is a suburban trunk-or-treat, recalibrate. This is a genuine six-week run of costume nights, a themed drag brunch with two full seatings, and a citywide bar crawl that happens to run right past the Arts District. Here is where to be, who is hosting what, and how to not show up underdressed.

Does Gay Tulsa Actually Do Halloween?

Enthusiastically, and with a budget for fake blood. Every one of Tulsa's three anchor gay bars runs a regular drag or theme night year-round, which means Halloween doesn't require anyone to invent new infrastructure. It just means the queens go bigger, the crowds show up in costume instead of their usual Friday best, and the calendar gets genuinely crowded for about three weekends straight.

The one honest caveat: bars in this city finalize their exact Halloween-week lineups close to the date, not six weeks out. Everything below is either a confirmed booking or a documented pattern from a venue that has run Halloween-themed nights before. Follow the Instagram accounts linked in each section, because that is where the specific October 2026 details land first.

Scream Queens: The Drag Brunch at Elote

This one is locked in, dated, and worth building your Saturday around. Elote's regular second-Saturday drag brunch goes full horror for October with a show called Scream Queens, running Saturday, October 10, 2026, with two seatings: 11am and 1:30pm. Costumes are explicitly welcome, which at a drag brunch means you should assume the bar for "dressed up" has been set considerably higher than whatever you were planning to throw on.

Elote Cafe & Catering 514 S Boston Ave, Tulsa Arts District Ghoul-glam queens, killer lewks, and Southwestern brunch food that has no business being this good next to a drag show. Book through Eventbrite before the 1:30 seating fills, because it will.

If you've never done a drag brunch in this city, our full Gayest Brunch guide covers the etiquette, the tipping math, and why you reserve ahead instead of walking in.

Club Majestic Goes Full Horror

Club Majestic's Thursday drag show runs every week of the year, doors at 9pm, show at 11pm, and the venue has a documented history of leaning into Halloween with full production numbers, including a ghost-themed drag show that made the rounds on their own social media. That is not speculation about what they might do this year. That is a venue that has already shown you what "Club Majestic does Halloween" looks like, and it looks expensive.

Club Majestic 124 N Boston Ave, Downtown Tulsa The flagship dance club and Tulsa's biggest drag stage. Expect the October Thursday shows, and very likely the weekend around the 31st itself, to be built around a costume or horror theme. Confirm the specific date on their Instagram before you plan your night around it, and get there after 10pm.

Tip your queens generously at a Halloween show specifically. Full prosthetics and a horror concept cost more in time and product than a regular Thursday number, and everyone working that stage knows it.

The Eagle and YBR on Halloween Weekend

The Tulsa Eagle runs drag on Fridays year-round, and a Halloween-weekend Friday at a leather bar in the Arts District is exactly the combination you'd hope for: horror camp meets leather crowd, no pretension, patio still open if the October night stays mild.

Tulsa Eagle 1338 E 3rd St, Arts District Open daily 2pm to 2am. Regular Friday drag, likely leaning horror through October. Check their Instagram for the exact Halloween-weekend lineup as the date gets closer.

Yellow Brick Road, meanwhile, does not need a theme to get chaotic on a Saturday night, but hand a room full of regulars a costume mandate and karaoke, and you have found the most sincerely fun Halloween night in the city that nobody outside the community knows to look for.

Yellow Brick Road (YBR) 2630 E 15th St, Cherry Street Karaoke, pool, darts, and a crowd that treats costume night as a personal challenge. Follow their Instagram for the specific Halloween-weekend schedule.

The Citywide Bar Crawl: Worth It or Skip It?

Tulsa also runs a citywide Halloween Bar Crawl on Saturday, October 31, from 3pm to 10pm, ticketed through Eventbrite. It is not a queer event, and it is not affiliated with any of the bars above. But the route runs through downtown and the Arts District, close enough to the Eagle that a savvy crawler can buy the ticket, hit the daytime stops, and peel off to the Eagle or Club Majestic once the sun goes down and the actual party starts. Treat it as a warm-up, not the main event.

What to Actually Wear

A costume, not a costume-adjacent gesture. Cat ears and a black shirt is not a costume, it's a Tuesday. This is a city where the drag queens on stage will have spent weeks on their look, and the room takes its cue from the stage. Commit to a concept, bring a jacket for the walk between bars because October nights here can turn cold fast, and wear shoes you can actually dance in, because you will be dancing.

If your costume involves anything that reads as a political statement about a real marginalized group played for a laugh, leave it at home. Every venue on this list has spent years building a room where people feel safe being exactly who they are. Don't be the reason that slips for one night.

Build Your Own Halloween Weekend

Saturday, October 10: Scream Queens drag brunch at Elote, 11am or 1:30pm. Book ahead. The weekend of the 31st: Club Majestic on Thursday or Friday for the horror-themed show, the Eagle Friday for drag with a leather-bar edge, YBR Saturday if karaoke and costumes sound like your idea of a good night, and the citywide bar crawl as a daytime warm-up if you want more bars on the route than three.

None of this requires you to pick just one night. Tulsa's queer Halloween calendar runs a full three weekends deep, and the city's small enough that you can hit all four venues without ever feeling rushed.
