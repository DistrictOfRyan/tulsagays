LGBTQ-Owned Businesses in Tulsa: 5 You Should Be Spending Your Money At Right Now

Not a listicle. An itinerary for where your money should already be going.

Every city has that one person at a networking mixer who will corner you, drink in hand, and explain at length why shopping local is a moral obligation. Normally you nod, agree in principle, and beeline for the cheese plate. In Tulsa, for once, that person is correct, they just never hand you the actual list. So here it is: five LGBTQ-owned and queer-friendly businesses in Tulsa that have earned your money on merit, not sympathy, plus exactly where to find the rest once these five aren't enough.

Why LGBTQ-Owned Businesses in Tulsa Deserve Your Money First

Oklahoma's legislature has spent the last several years making life measurably harder for queer people in this state, and no amount of brunch is going to undo that at the ballot box. What it can do is decide, dollar by dollar, which businesses get to keep their lights on. Oklahomans for Equality runs the Equality Business Alliance specifically for this reason, a membership network of LGBTQ-owned and LGBTQ-affirming businesses across the state that gets a public directory, networking mixers, and a reason to keep showing up for each other when the state capitol won't. Every business below either belongs on that list or should.

Il Seme: Queer-Owned Italian Worth the Reservation

Il Seme 15 W 5th St, Downtown Tulsa · Queer-Owned Regional Italian from James Beard-nominated chef Lisa Becklund and co-owner Linda Ford, the pair also behind FarmBar and Living Kitchen Farm & Dairy. Tuesday through Saturday dinner, with bar specials Tuesday through Friday.

Il Seme is the restaurant you send visiting family to when you want them to stop asking whether there's "actually anything good to eat" in Oklahoma. It is a genuinely excellent, sourced-from-the-farm-they-also-own Italian restaurant that happens to be run by two women, and it books up on weekends for reasons that have nothing to do with the politics of where you spend your cash and everything to do with the pasta. Reserve ahead. Order whatever's using the farm's own dairy that week. Follow @ilsemetulsa for the rotating menu.

Pony Coffee: The Queer-Owned Standard

Pony Coffee 1623 S Main St, Riverview · Queer-Owned Specialty espresso roasted in-house, a calm layout that treats a laptop table like furniture instead of a statement, and a Riverview location that stays quieter than the Arts District coffee shops during peak hours.

Pony Coffee already earned its own paragraph in our remote-work coffee shop guide, and it earns another one here for the same reason: it's queer-owned, the espresso is genuinely good rather than merely serviceable, and it's a five-minute walk from the Arts District bars if your afternoon coffee wants to turn into an evening out. If your money is funding somebody's caffeine habit anyway, let it be a queer somebody's.

The Donut Hole: The Bakery That Got Firebombed and Stayed Open

The Donut Hole 3305 S Peoria Ave, Brookside A Brookside doughnut shop with an inclusive, drag-friendly reputation, and a history that earned it a permanent place on lists like this one whether it planned to be here or not.

Here's the part of this guide that isn't cute. In October 2022, The Donut Hole hosted a grand-opening art installation featuring drag queens serving doughnut sculptures. Two weeks later, someone broke a window with a baseball bat and threw a Molotov cocktail through it, and anti-LGBTQ+ notes turned up at nearby businesses the same night. The shop had already been burglarized once that week. The owners' response, in full, was two words: "Love wins." A GoFundMe raised more than $14,000, and they pledged whatever was left over to Oklahomans for Equality's Equality Center. The Human Rights Campaign called it out publicly. The shop is still open, still selling doughnuts, still standing on South Peoria. That's not a reason to feel sorry for a bakery. It's a reason to buy a dozen.

Tonsorial Barbershop: Queer-Friendly Grooming Done Right

Tonsorial Barbershop 416 E 11th St, Downtown Tulsa (Route 66) · Queer-Friendly A traditional barbershop on historic Route 66 that has built a genuine reputation as a warm, affirming space for clients of every race, sexuality, and gender identity. Monday through Friday 9am to 6pm, Saturday 9am to 4pm.

Finding a barber who won't make small talk you have to carefully navigate is its own category of queer relief, and Tonsorial has become the answer a lot of Tulsa's queer community landed on independently, which is its own kind of endorsement. It's a proper barbershop, straight razor and all, not a gimmick with rainbow flags in the window. Book ahead on weekends. It's on Route 66, so make a morning of the strip while you're down there.

Jo & June Vintage: Tulsa's Favorite Queer-Friendly Thrift

Jo & June Vintage 2413 E Admiral Blvd, Kendall-Whittier · Queer-Friendly A vintage and antique shop run by Tulsa natives Philip and Danielle since 2015, carrying curated vintage furniture, Made-In-Oklahoma goods, and the kind of curiosities you don't plan on buying and then absolutely do.

Jo & June shows up on Tulsa's LGBTQ+ Pride Month business lists every year for good reason, and once you've wandered its Kendall-Whittier storefront you'll understand why people drive across town for a candle. It's the rare vintage shop that's genuinely well-curated rather than just crowded, and it's a five-minute walk from She Brews Coffee Roasters if you want to turn a shopping trip into a proper afternoon out.

More LGBTQ-Owned Businesses in Tulsa (And Where to Find the Rest)

Five businesses is a start, not the ceiling. Oklahomans for Equality maintains a running Food & Drink directory with dozens of LGBTQ-owned and affirming restaurants, bars, and bakeries across the metro, and their Equality Business Alliance is the membership network behind it, worth joining if you own a business here yourself. For everything beyond food and drink, our own LGBTQ+ Business Directory rounds out the picture.

None of these five businesses needed Oklahoma's political climate to try this hard. They did it anyway, some of them literally rebuilding after someone tried to burn them down for it. The least this deserves is your next dinner reservation, your next haircut, and your next cup of coffee going somewhere that's earned it.
