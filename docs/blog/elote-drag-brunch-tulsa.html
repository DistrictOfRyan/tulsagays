ELOTE DRAG BRUNCH: WHAT IT IS, HOW TO GET A TABLE, AND WHY YOU NEED TO GO

Oklahoma's longest-running drag brunch happens once a month in a Mexican restaurant downtown, and it sells out for reasons that will make complete sense the moment the first puffy taco lands on your table.

Every city with a functioning gay scene has a drag brunch. Very few of them have been doing it long enough to earn the word "institution," and Tulsa's does not get nearly enough credit for holding that title. Every second Saturday of the month, a Mexican restaurant on South Boston Avenue clears its dining room, brings in a stage's worth of talent, and runs Oklahoma's longest-running drag brunch to two sold-out seatings while most of downtown is still nursing its Friday night. If you have lived in this city for any real stretch of time and have not been, that is a gap in your Tulsa education, and it is an easy one to close.

This is Elote Cafe & Catering, at 514 S Boston Ave in the Deco District downtown, and this is everything you need to know before you show up.

"Puffy tacos, mimosas, a rotating cast of queens who have clearly been doing this a while, and a room full of people who booked ahead because they already know better than to walk in cold."

What Elote's Drag Brunch Actually Is

Elote runs the show as two distinct seatings, one at 11am and a second at 1:30pm, on the second Saturday of every month. That is not a soft "usually" schedule that shifts around depending on whoever they could book; it is a standing monthly production with a rotating cast of drag performers pulled from across the region, which is exactly why it has stayed the longest-running drag brunch in the state while other one-off attempts around Tulsa have come and gone. Two shows a month does not sound like much until you realize that is 24 opportunities a year for a queen to work a Tulsa crowd, and this crowd has had plenty of practice being worked.

The format is the one you already know if you have ever done a drag brunch anywhere else, just executed with more consistency than most: numbers between courses, tip buckets that circulate with real purpose, and a host who treats a table of regulars celebrating a birthday like the main event of the day, because for that table, it is. What sets Elote's version apart is the setting. This is a full restaurant that reorganizes itself around the show, not a bar with a folding stage wedged into a corner, which means better sightlines, actual table service, and a menu built by people who cook for a living.

How to Get a Table Before It Sells Out

This is the part people get wrong, and it is the entire reason this section exists. Elote's Drag Brunch is not a walk-in situation. Tickets go on sale ahead of each second Saturday through Eventbrite or directly on elotetulsa.com/events, and both shows have a real habit of selling out, particularly the earlier 11am seating. If you are the type who likes to decide on a Friday night that Saturday brunch sounds fun, you will need to break that habit specifically for this event.

Bar seating runs around $13 a head, which is about the price of a mediocre cocktail anywhere else in this city, and table seating is priced by group size, so a party of six is not paying six times a solo rate for no reason. Here is the detail that trips up first-timers every single month: the ticket price gets you the seat and the show. It does not include brunch. You order and pay for your actual food once you are seated, the same way you would at any restaurant, so budget for both the ticket and the meal rather than assuming one covers the other.

Book the seating you actually want, not whatever happened to still be available when you finally remembered to look. Both shows run the same production with different casts and a different room energy. Early risers who want brunch to still feel like a morning event should take the 11am. Anyone who would rather ease into a Saturday should take the 1:30.

The Food, Because You Will Ask

Elote's signature move is the puffy taco, a Tex-Mex staple that most Tulsa restaurants do not bother attempting because it is genuinely harder to execute well than a regular taco shell suggests. Paired with a full mimosa program, it is the reason regulars will tell you the food is not an afterthought to the drag, it is the second reason they keep coming back. This is not a brunch where you are settling for a rubbery buffet chafing dish while you wait for the next number. The kitchen is running a real Mexican and Southwestern menu the entire time, and the drag brunch crowd gets the same food quality as anyone walking in for a Tuesday lunch, just with considerably more glitter in the room.

Come hungry. A ticket price that does not include the meal is doing you a favor, not a disservice, because it means you are ordering off a real menu rather than picking at a pre-set tray that was decided by committee three weeks ago.

Who Can Actually Go

The show itself is all ages welcome, which matters more than it sounds like it should. Drag brunches in a lot of cities quietly become 21+ affairs by default, and that shuts out the exact people who benefit most from seeing a well-run, joyful drag show in a safe daytime setting. Elote does not do that. Families come. Kids come. The one carve-out is that bartop seating and the champagne service specifically are 21 and over, so if you are bringing a table that includes anyone under 21, seat them away from the bar and everyone has a perfectly good time anyway.

This also makes Elote's Drag Brunch one of the easiest on-ramps in the city for someone who has never seen drag in person. It is daytime, it is a restaurant, and there is a menu you can read. Nobody is asking you to commit to a 10pm cover charge and a room you cannot see the exits of. If you have a friend, a parent, or a coworker who keeps saying they are "curious" about drag but has never taken the leap, this is where you take them.

Why This Beats Every Other Drag Show in Town

Tulsa's drag scene is genuinely strong. Club Majestic runs a weekly show, Yellow Brick Road hosts drag nights of its own, and the Eagle brings drag into a bar built around a completely different mood. Elote is not trying to out-produce any of them. It occupies the one lane none of them can touch: a full sit-down restaurant experience, in daylight, running long enough and consistently enough to have earned the "longest-running" title outright.

That consistency is the actual selling point. A one-off pop-up drag brunch is a nice idea that might happen again next year if the organizers have the energy. Elote's has been happening on a fixed schedule long enough that you can plan around it the way you plan around a monthly standing dinner with friends, except this one comes with a stage, a host working the room, and a tip bucket that is doing real work for the performers passing it around. Put it on the calendar the second Saturday of every month and stop treating it as a special occasion. It already is one, every single time.

"This is the version of drag brunch you bring your mother to. It is also the version you bring your hangover to. It handles both assignments equally well."

Quick Facts: Elote's Drag Brunch

  • Venue: Elote Cafe & Catering, 514 S Boston Ave, Tulsa, OK 74103 (Deco District, downtown)
  • Schedule: Every second Saturday of the month, two shows: 11:00 AM and 1:30 PM
  • Tickets: Via Eventbrite or elotetulsa.com/events; bar seating ~$13, table seating priced by group size
  • Food: Ordered and paid for separately from the ticket, once seated. Signature dish: puffy tacos. Full mimosa program.
  • Age: All ages welcome to the show. Bartop seating and champagne service are 21+ only.
  • Website: elotetulsa.com
  • Instagram: @elotetulsa
  • Book ahead: This sells out, especially the 11am seating. Do not show up unticketed and expect a seat.

The Honest Summary

Elote's Drag Brunch is not a secret and it should not stay one. It is a monthly, second-Saturday, two-show production at a downtown restaurant that treats both the drag and the food like they matter, and Oklahoma does not have another drag brunch that has stuck around this long for a reason. Buy your ticket ahead of time, pick the seating that matches how your Saturday actually goes, and leave room on the bill for a puffy taco you will be describing to friends for weeks afterward.
