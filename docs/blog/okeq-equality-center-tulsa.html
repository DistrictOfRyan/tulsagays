The Equality Center: What OkEq Actually Does in Tulsa

45 years old, 18,000 square feet, and most of you have driven past it without knowing what's inside.

July 22, 2026 · Community Guide · 8 min read
🕒 8 min read · Published July 22, 2026

Here is a fact that should embarrass every gay person who has lived in Tulsa longer than a year: there is an 18,000-square-foot LGBTQ+ community center sitting at 621 E 4th Street, it has been open in some form since 1996, and a startling number of you have never walked through the door. You have seen the building. You have maybe even parked near it for Club Majestic. You did not go in. That ends today.

Oklahomans for Equality, universally shortened to OkEq because Tulsa loves an acronym, is not a museum piece or a relic of scrappier decades. It is a working, funded, staffed organization running a health clinic, a weekly support group, a lending library, and the closest thing this city has to a queer town hall. It has also been doing this since before most of Tulsa's current gay bars existed, which earns it a certain authority nobody argues with.

What Is OkEq, Actually?

OkEq started in 1980 under the name Tulsa Oklahomans for Human Rights, back when saying that name out loud in Oklahoma took actual nerve. In 1996 the group opened Oklahoma's first dedicated LGBT community center, and after an eight-year capital campaign that would test anyone's patience, they bought the building they still occupy. The Dennis R. Neill Equality Center opened its doors in February 2007, and it remains one of the largest LGBTQ+ centers in the entire region, not just the state.

The mission, in plain language: equal rights and real services for the 2SLGBTQIA+ community across Oklahoma, delivered through advocacy, education, and a building full of programs that actually help people on a Tuesday afternoon, not just at a rally. That distinction matters. A lot of orgs talk. OkEq runs a clinic.

Inside the Building: What 18,000 Square Feet of Equality Looks Like

Eighteen thousand square feet is a lot of building, and OkEq uses every inch of it. There is a lending library you can wander into without an appointment. There is an art gallery that rotates local queer artists, so it functions as a genuine cultural stop and not just a place with pamphlets in the lobby. There is an event center that hosts everything from Pride programming to community meetings, a wellness space, and a kitchen that gets put to good use when there's a gala or a potluck, and there is always a gala or a potluck.

Dennis R. Neill Equality Center 621 E 4th St, Tulsa, OK · Tue-Thu 9am-9pm, Fri 9am-6pm, Sat 12pm-6pm, Sun-Mon closed · (918) 743-4297 The physical home of Oklahomans for Equality. Walk in during open hours, no appointment needed, unless you're headed to the health clinic below.

The building also functions as meeting space for a rotating cast of community groups, everything from support circles to sports league organizing, which means the calendar at okeq.org/event-calendar is worth checking before you assume nothing is happening on a random Wednesday. Something almost always is.

The Health Clinic

This is the part people are least likely to know about and most likely to need. The OkEq Health Clinic offers HRT, PrEP and PEP, and STI and HIV screening for the 2SLGBTQIA+ community, ages 18 and up. It runs Tuesday and Wednesday 9am to 4pm, Thursday 9am to noon, and Friday 1pm to 4pm, and you need an appointment at least 24 hours ahead by calling 918-938-6537. That is a separate number from the front desk, so save both.

For a lot of Tulsans, especially anyone newly out, newly on hormones, or just tired of explaining themselves to a provider who doesn't get it, this clinic is the whole reason OkEq matters more than any bar in town. Nobody has to perform anything here. That is the entire point.

The Gender Support Group

Every Wednesday from 7:00 to 8:30pm, OkEq runs a Gender Support Group at the Equality Center. On the second Wednesday of each month, it expands into breakout groups sorted by identity: non-binary, trans men, trans women, and partners of trans people, so you're not stuck in one room trying to make your very specific experience fit everyone else's. First-timers need a brief intake interview before their first session, which sounds bureaucratic and is actually a kindness. It means the room stays a room people can trust. Call 918-743-4297 to set that up.

We cover the full landscape of trans resources across Tulsa, including this group, in our complete trans resources guide, if you need the wider picture beyond OkEq's walls.

The Faith Directory

Here's a service most visitors never think to look for: OkEq maintains a running directory of affirming faith communities across Tulsa, the actual churches and synagogues that will marry you, baptize your kid, and not flinch, rather than the ones that just put a rainbow sticker in the window during June and take it down in July. We built our own guide to affirming churches in Tulsa using OkEq's list as the verified source, because when a 45-year-old advocacy organization vouches for a congregation, that vouching means something.

How to Actually Use This Place

You do not need a crisis to walk into the Equality Center. You do not need to be in the middle of a coming-out story or a health emergency or a legal fight. You can walk in because you're new to Tulsa and want to meet people who aren't strangers from an app. You can walk in because the gallery show looks interesting. You can walk in because you saw this article and felt a little guilty about never having gone. All of those reasons are correct reasons.

Follow @okeqtulsa on Instagram or their Facebook page for the running calendar, and check okeq.org/event-calendar directly before you plan a visit around a specific program, since clinic hours and group schedules are worth confirming close to the date. And if OkEq's own calendar has a queer event that isn't already on ours, submit it to us so the rest of Tulsa hears about it too. OkEq itself, plus dozens of other affirming orgs and LGBTQ-owned businesses, are listed in our LGBTQ+ Tulsa directory.

Forty-five years is a long time to keep a promise to a community that Oklahoma has not always made it easy to serve. OkEq kept it anyway, and built 18,000 square feet of proof. The least you can do is go see it.
