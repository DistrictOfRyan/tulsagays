Getting Sober in Tulsa: LGBTQ+ Recovery Resources That Actually Help

Affirming meetings, real treatment, and crisis lines that still answer, no flinching required.

Let's say the sentence nobody puts on a pamphlet: getting sober is hard enough without also wondering whether the person next to you in the circle is going to flinch when you mention your husband. That is not a hypothetical. It is the exact, specific reason a meeting that works beautifully for one person sends the next person straight back out the door before the serenity prayer finishes. Tulsa has real recovery infrastructure for LGBTQ+ people. It is just not always labeled loudly enough to find on the night you actually need it, so here it is, labeled, verified, and without a single fabricated meeting time.

Is There an LGBTQ+-Specific AA or NA Meeting in Tulsa?

There is not one single, permanently scheduled queer-only meeting sitting on a church calendar waiting for you, and I am not going to invent one just to give you a tidier answer. What exists instead is better than a stale list: AA Oklahoma's own online meeting finder lets you filter results by "LGBTQIAA+" as a meeting type, the same tagging standard used by AA groups nationwide, so you pull a live, current result instead of trusting anyone's blog post, mine included, six months from now. Gay & Sober, the national nonprofit meeting finder built specifically for LGBTQ+ people in recovery, maintains an Oklahoma page that routes you straight to that filtered list for the Tulsa area. Use both. Meeting rooms move, times shift, and the people running them organize for each other, not for search engines. The filter is how you get let in on it.

AA Oklahoma Meeting Guide Filter by LGBTQIAA+ meeting type at aaoklahoma.org for a live, current list rather than a fixed schedule that will be wrong by the time you read it.
Gay & Sober National LGBTQ+ recovery meeting finder with a dedicated Oklahoma page at gayandsober.org. Built by and for people in recovery who wanted a room where they didn't have to come out twice.

OkEq's Substance Use Program: The Front Door You Didn't Know Had a Back Room

Everyone in queer Tulsa knows Oklahomans for Equality as the org that runs Pride and the building downtown with the flag out front. Fewer know it as a health provider. The Dennis R. Neill Equality Center at 621 E 4th St runs an actual health clinic, and tucked into its services list, right alongside the gender support group and HIV testing, sit Substance Use services and Medication Assisted Recovery. This is not a folding-chairs-and-drip-coffee support group bolted on as an afterthought. It is licensed care from an organization that has spent 45 years learning how to talk to queer people without making them explain themselves first.

Dennis R. Neill Equality Center Health Clinic 621 E 4th St, Tulsa, OK 74120 Clinic line: 918-938-6537. Main line: 918-743-4297. Open Tuesday through Thursday 9am to 9pm, Friday 9am to 6pm, Saturday noon to 6pm, closed Sunday and Monday. Call and ask what substance use services and Medication Assisted Recovery slots are currently available.

12 and 12: Tulsa's Detox and Treatment Center, Under a New Name

If a meeting or an outpatient support group isn't enough, and for plenty of people it genuinely isn't, Tulsa's long-standing detox and treatment center still exists, though it's had a rebrand. Known to three generations of locals simply as 12 and 12, it merged with GRAND Mental Health in 2022 and now operates as GRAND Addiction Recovery Center. Don't be surprised if the intake coordinator on the phone still calls it 12 and 12, because everyone does, out of habit and a little bit of affection. It runs detox and treatment programming and lists LGBTQ+ people, alongside people managing HIV or AIDS, dual mental health diagnoses, and pregnant or postpartum patients, among the populations it treats. Confirm those specifics directly with intake before you commit, because "we treat everyone" and "we treat you well" are two different promises, and you deserve the second one, not just the first.

What to Do Tonight If You're in Crisis, Not Just Sober-Curious

If tonight is the night things feel unmanageable and not a night for research, skip everything above and call one of these instead. Straightforward, current, and checked, not assumed.

The Trevor Project 24/7 crisis line built specifically for LGBTQ+ young people ages 13 to 24. Call 1-866-488-7386, or text START to 678678.
Trans Lifeline Peer support line staffed by trans people for trans and questioning callers, no age limit. Call 877-565-8860. You don't need to be in crisis to call, you're allowed to call just to talk to someone trans.
988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline Still answers 24/7 by call or text to 988. Its dedicated LGBTQ+ youth "press 3" option was defunded and shut down in July 2025, so don't go looking for it, it's genuinely gone, and that's worth being angry about on a day you have the energy for it.

Why "Just Find a Regular Meeting" Isn't Actually Helpful Advice

Here's the part most well-meaning pamphlets skip. Oklahoma's political climate has given plenty of queer and trans people real reasons to be careful about who they come out to in a room full of strangers, even a room built around honesty. That caution is not a character flaw, and it is not "resistance to recovery." It is a rational response to a state that has, in fact, earned the caution. The fix isn't gritting through the first meeting on a church bulletin board. It's using the filters and organizations above, built by people who already did the work of making the room safer, so you don't have to test that safety yourself on day one.

Start Somewhere Tonight

Recovery resources in Tulsa exist, they're funded, they're staffed, and increasingly they know how to talk to you like a whole person instead of a diagnosis with a rainbow sticker on it. Start with OkEq if you want a human being in a building. Start with Gay & Sober's filtered meeting list if you want a seat in a circle. Start with the Trevor Project or Trans Lifeline tonight if you can't wait for either. None of them require you to have it figured out first. Our full breakdown of what OkEq actually does and our guide to trans resources in Tulsa cover the surrounding support systems in more depth, because recovery rarely happens in isolation from everything else going on in a life.
